Surveillance text, in the wild

Most outbreaks are first visible as informal reports, not case counts.

A news article, a ministry bulletin, a local clinician's post, often days or weeks before a formal surveillance system reports anything.

Event-based surveillance means monitoring these informal signals directly, at a volume no team can read by hand.

Case study

BEACON

An LLM triages web signals in minutes. A person still signs off on every report.

Web signals via HealthMap → LLM: translate, triage, draft → Expert review → Published report

Drafted in minutes, in seven languages. Nothing publishes without a subject-matter expert's approval.

Bhadelia, Paschalidis, Brownstein & Lassmann, “A BEACON for Novel Disease Threats: Leveraging Artificial Intelligence for Informal Event-Based Outbreak Surveillance,” The Journal of Infectious Diseases, 2026.

The lesson

Speed comes from the LLM. Trust comes from the reviewer.

BEACON's designers didn't automate away the expert; they used the LLM to get a plausible draft in front of one, fast. That's a pattern worth keeping in mind for the rest of this section.
